What You're Actually Buying

A guide for apartment buyers and renters in Sydney.
The legal minimum is routinely treated as the maximum you will get.

50m²

Minimum one-bedroom apartment size under the Apartment Design Guide. Most developer apartments in Sydney are built to this number exactly.

70m² two-bedroom

Minimum two-bedroom apartment size. A bedroom can meet its benchmark and still not accept a bed with the wardrobe open.

33%

Increase in psychological distress risk for every additional building defect reported by apartment residents. (RMIT, 2022)

In Sydney's developer-led apartment market, the legal minimum benchmarks for apartment size, natural light, ventilation and communal space are routinely treated as the maximum you will get. Every square metre above the minimum is subtracted from the developer's return. This guide explains what that means for the apartment you are buying or renting, and what to look for before you commit.

What is the Apartment Design Guide?

The Apartment Design Guide (ADG) is a NSW planning instrument that sets minimum benchmarks for apartment design. It specifies requirements for apartment size, ceiling height, solar access, natural ventilation, private open space, communal open space, and storage. It was introduced in 2015 under State Environmental Planning Policy 65 (first made in 2002, now part of the Housing SEPP), replacing the earlier Residential Flat Design Code, to improve the quality of apartment living in New South Wales.

The benchmarks are minimums, not targets. They describe the least an apartment is allowed to provide, not what it should aspire to. In practice, these minimums are routinely treated as the specification.

How to read a floor plan

Four checks to make before you trust the plan in the brochure.

1

Check the scale

Every floor plan should include a scale bar. Use it to check actual room dimensions rather than relying on the proportions of the image. Without a scale bar, you cannot tell whether a room is 3 metres wide or 4.

2

Measure the furniture

Marketing floor plans frequently use undersized furniture to make rooms appear larger. A real queen bed is 1.53 x 2.03m. Measure the bedroom on the plan and check whether the bed fits with space to open the wardrobe and walk around it.

3

Check the ceiling height

The ADG minimum ceiling height is 2.7m for habitable rooms. Ask whether your apartment meets this or exceeds it. A 2.7m ceiling in a room proportioned to the minimum feels noticeably compressed. Every centimetre above the minimum changes the experience of the space.

4

Check the depth

How deep is the apartment from the window wall to the back of the plan? Natural light falls off sharply after 6 to 8 metres. If the apartment is deeper than 8m from a window, the rooms at the back will receive very little natural light regardless of the orientation shown in the marketing material.
